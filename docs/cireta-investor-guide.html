A regulated RWA tokenization launchpad that issues ERC-3643 security tokens backed by physical commodities. Every token represents a fractional ownership of a verified real-world asset.
⛓️
Base L2 Blockchain
All tokens live on Base — Coinbase's Ethereum Layer 2. Transactions are fast (2s finality), low cost (<$0.01), and secured by Ethereum's full validator set.

KYC Verification
Know Your Customer (KYC) verification is a legal requirement for investing in regulated securities. Cireta uses Sumsub, an industry-leading identity verification platform, to process KYC safely and quickly.
launchpad.cireta.com/kyc

KYC Levels

Level 1 — Identity
Required to view
Government-issued photo ID (passport, national ID, or driver's licence). Required to browse restricted project details.
Level 2 — Address
Required to invest
Proof of address: utility bill or bank statement dated within 3 months. Required to make contributions and request redemptions.

Process Steps

1
Start KYC
Navigate to Account → KYC Status and click Start Verification.
2
Document Upload
Upload clear photos of your ID and proof of address via the Sumsub widget.
3
Liveness Check
Complete a brief selfie/liveness check. Takes ~30 seconds.
4
Review (1–24h)
Sumsub reviews your submission. You will be notified by email when approved.
✓ Your Data is Protected
Your KYC data is encrypted at rest using AES-256 and transmitted over TLS 1.3. Cireta stores only an anonymized reference ID — your full documents are held by Sumsub under their GDPR-compliant data retention policies. You may request deletion at any time.

CIRETA Chapter 08 — Direct vs. Vested Sales Page 10
Chapter 08
Direct vs. Vested Sales
Cireta offers two sale types. Direct sales deliver tokens immediately at claim. Vested sales release tokens gradually over a predetermined schedule — often offering a better price in exchange for the lockup period.
Option A
Direct Sale
  • Tokens delivered immediately at claim
  • No lockup period
  • Standard market price
  • Full liquidity from day one
  • Suitable for shorter investment horizons
  • Claim available after sale finalization
Option B
Vested Sale
  • Tokens released on a schedule (e.g., 25% / quarter)
  • Lockup period defined by issuer
  • Typically discounted token price
  • Encourages long-term holding
  • Suitable for strategic investors
  • Each tranche claimable individually

Claiming Vested Tranches
When a vesting tranche becomes claimable, you will see a Claim button appear in your Portfolio → Vesting tab. Each tranche must be claimed individually. Unclaimed tranches accumulate — they do not expire. Gas fees on Base are minimal (typically < $0.01 per claim).

CIRETA Chapter 10 — Dividends & Distributions Page 12
Chapter 10
Dividends & Distributions
Some tokenized assets generate revenue — from storage leasing, futures roll yield, or commodity-backed income streams. Issuers may distribute this income to token holders in the form of USDC dividends.
💰
How Dividends Work
The issuer deposits USDC into the token's dividend contract. Distributions are proportional to your token holdings at the snapshot date.
📅
Snapshot Date
A snapshot is taken on the declared record date. Only holders with tokens in their registered wallet at that block are eligible for the distribution.
🏦
Claiming Dividends
After the distribution is declared, navigate to Portfolio → Holdings → select the token → Claim Dividends. USDC is sent to your registered wallet.

Dividend Eligibility Rules

  • Tokens must be in a registered and KYC-approved wallet at the snapshot block
  • Tokens held in unregistered wallets are not eligible
  • Vested but unclaimed tokens do not qualify — only delivered tokens in your wallet
  • Compliance-frozen wallets are excluded from distributions
Dividend Notifications
You will receive an email notification when a new dividend is declared for a token you hold. A banner will also appear on your portfolio dashboard. Dividends do not expire, but we recommend claiming promptly.
ⓘ Tax Considerations
Dividend income from tokenized assets may be subject to tax in your jurisdiction. Cireta provides a transaction history export (CSV) from your account settings to assist with tax reporting. Consult a qualified tax advisor for guidance specific to your situation.

CIRETA Chapter 13 — Security Best Practices Page 15
Chapter 13
Security Best Practices
Protecting your account and wallet is your responsibility. Follow these guidelines to keep your investments safe.

Account Security

🔑 Use a Strong Password
At least 12 characters, mixed case, numbers, and symbols. Use a dedicated password manager (1Password, Bitwarden) rather than browser-saved passwords.
✉ Protect Your Email
Your email is the recovery mechanism for your account. Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) on your email provider. Use a dedicated email address for crypto accounts.
🔒 Verify Sessions
Regularly review active sessions in Account Settings. Log out of sessions you don't recognise. Always log out on shared or public computers.

Wallet Security

📜 Seed Phrase is Sacred
Write your seed phrase on paper and store it in a secure physical location (safe, safety deposit box). Never store it digitally — no photos, no cloud, no email.
🛡 Consider a Hardware Wallet
For significant holdings, use a hardware wallet (Ledger, Trezor). These keep your private key offline and require physical confirmation for every transaction.
🔍 Verify Every Transaction
Always read transaction prompts in your wallet carefully. Verify the contract address and amount before signing. Cireta will never ask you to approve unexpected transactions.
⚠ Phishing Warning
Cireta will NEVER contact you asking for your password, seed phrase, or private key. We will NEVER DM you on Telegram, Discord, or social media offering investment opportunities. If anyone claiming to be Cireta staff asks for sensitive information, report it immediately to security@cireta.com.

CIRETA Chapter 14 — FAQ (Part 1) Page 16
Chapter 14
Frequently Asked Questions
Answers to the most common investor questions. Part 1 of 2.
Q1. What is a security token and how is it different from a cryptocurrency?
A security token is a blockchain-based digital asset that represents ownership or rights in a real-world asset (in Cireta's case, physical commodities). Unlike cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin or Ether, security tokens are regulated financial instruments subject to securities law and require investor KYC. They use the ERC-3643 standard which enforces compliance rules at the smart-contract level.
Q2. Which countries can invest on Cireta?
Cireta accepts investors from most jurisdictions, subject to local securities law. Residents of sanctioned countries (OFAC list) and certain restricted jurisdictions are not eligible. The platform enforces this at the KYC screening stage via Sumsub's AML/sanctions database.
Q3. How long does KYC take?
Automated KYC typically completes in 1–3 minutes via the Sumsub widget. Cases requiring manual review take 24–48 hours. You will receive an email notification when your status changes.
Q4. What currency do I use to invest?
All contributions are made in USDC (USD Coin) on the Base network. You will need USDC in your registered wallet before contributing to any sale. USDC can be acquired on centralised exchanges (Coinbase, Binance) and bridged to Base.
Q5. Are there minimum or maximum investment amounts?
Each sale defines its own minimum contribution (visible on the sale page). There is typically no platform-wide maximum, but individual issuers may set per-investor caps for regulatory compliance. Minimum investments typically range from $50 to $500 USDC.
Q6. Can I transfer my tokens to another wallet?
ERC-3643 security tokens can only be transferred between wallets that are KYC-approved and whitelisted on the token's on-chain identity registry. You cannot freely transfer tokens to arbitrary addresses — the compliance layer will reject the transaction.
Q7. What happens if the sale doesn't reach its target?
If the sale does not reach its minimum funding threshold, the issuer will mark it as failed and all contributors may claim a full refund of their USDC contribution. No fees are charged on failed sales. Refunds are available immediately after the sale is marked as failed.
Q8. How is the token price determined?
Token prices are set by the issuer at sale creation, reflecting the underlying asset's market value. After the sale, token NAV is tracked via Chainlink Proof of Reserve price feeds, which are updated on-chain at regular intervals. Redemption prices use the Chainlink price at the time of request.

CIRETA Chapter 15 — FAQ (Part 2) Page 17
Chapter 15
Frequently Asked Questions
Answers to common investor questions. Part 2 of 2.
Q9. What is Proof of Reserve and why does it matter?
Proof of Reserve (PoR) is an on-chain attestation that the physical commodity backing the token actually exists and is held in a verified vault. Cireta integrates Chainlink PoR feeds so investors can independently verify that the issued token supply is fully backed at any time.
Q10. What fees does Cireta charge?
Platform fees are set per-issuer and are visible on each project page before you invest. Fees are charged as a percentage of the raise (default 2%, or 200 basis points). There are no additional hidden fees. Gas fees on Base are minimal — typically under $0.01 per transaction.
Q11. Can my wallet be frozen and why?
Yes, wallets can be frozen by the issuer or platform compliance team if there is a regulatory requirement (e.g., court order, sanctions screening hit, AML investigation). Frozen wallets cannot receive, transfer, or redeem tokens until unfrozen. You will be notified if this occurs and may appeal through legal channels.
Q12. How do I claim my tokens after a sale finalizes?
After finalization, a Claim button appears in your Portfolio for the relevant sale. Click it, confirm the transaction in your wallet, and tokens will be transferred to your primary registered wallet. Claiming requires a small gas fee paid in ETH on Base (typically < $0.01).
Q13. What if I lose access to my registered wallet?
If you lose access to your wallet, the issuer has a compliance-controlled recovery mechanism. Contact support with proof of identity. The issuer can use a forced transfer (on-chain) to recover your tokens to a new wallet, following their compliance process. This is why keeping your seed phrase safe is critical.
Q14. Are security tokens tradeable on exchanges?
ERC-3643 tokens can only be traded on regulated security token exchanges that integrate with ONCHAINID for compliance. They cannot be listed on standard DEXes (Uniswap, etc.) as the smart contract enforces transfer restrictions. Cireta may announce secondary market partnerships in the future.
Q15. How do I report a problem or dispute?
Use the in-app Help & Support section or email investor-support@cireta.com. For compliance-related matters, contact compliance@cireta.com. For urgent security issues, email security@cireta.com. All disputes are logged and tracked with a ticket reference number provided by return email.

CIRETA Chapter 16 — Glossary (A–L) Page 18
Chapter 16
Glossary A–L
Key terms used throughout the Cireta platform and this guide.
AML (Anti-Money Laundering)
Regulatory framework requiring financial institutions to detect and prevent the proceeds of crime from entering the financial system.
Base
An Ethereum Layer 2 (L2) blockchain developed by Coinbase. Provides fast, low-cost transactions secured by Ethereum's mainnet.
Chainlink PoR
Chainlink Proof of Reserve — decentralised oracle network data feed confirming that a tokenized asset is backed by real-world reserves.
Compliance Freeze
A regulatory action that prevents a wallet from transferring or receiving security tokens. Enacted by the issuer or platform in response to legal requirements.
ERC-3643
An Ethereum token standard for compliant security tokens. Enforces transfer restrictions and identity verification at the smart-contract level.
Forced Transfer
A compliance action allowing an issuer to move tokens from one address to another, typically used for wallet recovery or regulatory orders.
Gas Fee
A small fee paid in ETH to the Base network to process a blockchain transaction. On Base, gas fees are typically under $0.01.
Identity Registry
An on-chain smart contract that maintains the list of KYC-approved wallet addresses permitted to hold a given security token.
IPFS
InterPlanetary File System — a distributed storage protocol. Cireta stores legal documents and token prospectuses on IPFS for immutable public access.
Issuer
A regulated legal entity that creates and manages tokenized assets on the Cireta platform. Issuers undergo their own KYB (Know Your Business) verification.
JWT (JSON Web Token)
A cryptographically signed token used for authentication. Cireta uses short-lived access JWTs (15 minutes) with rotating refresh tokens (7 days).
KYC (Know Your Customer)
The process of verifying the identity of an investor. Required by law for regulated securities platforms. Cireta uses Sumsub for KYC processing.
Layer 2 (L2)
A secondary blockchain built on top of Ethereum that processes transactions faster and cheaper while inheriting Ethereum's security guarantees.
Liveness Check
A brief video selfie performed during KYC to confirm that the person submitting documents is physically present and matches the provided ID.

CIRETA Chapter 17 — Glossary (M–Z) Page 19
Chapter 17
Glossary M–Z
Continued glossary of key platform terms.
NAV (Net Asset Value)
The value per token calculated from the current market price of the underlying commodity, divided by total token supply. Used for redemption pricing.
ONCHAINID
A decentralised identity standard on Ethereum. Cireta uses ONCHAINID to bind KYC approval to a wallet address on-chain, enabling compliant token transfers.
Proof of Reserve (PoR)
On-chain attestation via Chainlink that the physical asset backing a token is held in custody. Investors can verify the reserve amount independently.
RWA (Real World Asset)
Any tangible or financial asset from the traditional world — gold bullion, copper, real estate, bonds — that has been tokenized on a blockchain.
Sale Contract
An audited smart contract deployed by the issuer that holds USDC contributions during a token sale and enforces fundraising rules on-chain.
Security Token
A blockchain-based token that is classified as a financial security under applicable law. Subject to regulatory oversight and investor protection rules.
Sumsub
The KYC/AML verification provider used by Cireta. Sumsub processes identity documents, performs liveness checks, and screens against sanctions lists.
Token Claim
The action of retrieving your purchased tokens from the sale contract to your wallet after a sale finalizes. Requires a small gas fee on Base.
Token Symbol
A short ticker code identifying a security token (e.g., CGLD for Cireta Gold). Used in portfolio displays and on-chain logs.
Tranche
A portion of vested tokens that becomes claimable at a specific date in a vesting schedule. Each tranche must be claimed individually.
USDC
USD Coin — a fully regulated, dollar-pegged stablecoin issued by Circle. Used as the contribution and settlement currency on the Cireta platform.
Vesting Schedule
A predetermined token release timeline for vested sale investors. Tokens unlock in tranches over time, incentivising long-term holding.
WalletConnect
An open-source protocol for connecting crypto wallets to web applications. Cireta supports WalletConnect v2 for wallet integration.
Whitelist
The on-chain list of KYC-approved wallet addresses permitted to hold a security token. Only whitelisted addresses can receive, hold, and transfer the token.
